# Ductus Definition

From HMML School Lexicon:

**Core Definition:**
"The number and direction of strokes used to form letters in a given script or hand, and their order of execution."

**Expanded Explanation:**
Ductus encompasses what scribes actually did while writing—the physical actions that produced the letters visible on the page. This includes three key components:

1. **Number of strokes** — how many individual marks comprise each letter
2. **Direction of strokes** — the path and orientation of each mark
3. **Order of execution** — the sequence in which strokes were applied

**Fundamental to Paleography:**
Understanding ductus allows scholars to analyze writing techniques and identify characteristics of different scripts and hands across various time periods and languages.

**Etymology:**
The term 'duct' or ductus comes from Latin meaning 'leading' or 'conduct'.

**Practical Relevance:**
A single letter may have distinctly different ductus across different scripts. For example, the letter 'o' in one script may be made in a single, circular stroke, while in another it may be made of six individual angular strokes. This illustrates how different scripts and hands can have distinctly different ductus for the same letter.
