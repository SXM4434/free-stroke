# Chinese Character Stroke Order Rules

The fundamental rules governing stroke order in Chinese character writing:

## 12 Rules

1. **Top to Bottom** – Write upper strokes before lower ones (e.g., 二)

2. **Left to Right** – Write left components before right ones (e.g., 一)

3. **Horizontal Before Vertical** – Horizontal strokes precede vertical strokes when crossing (e.g., 十)

4. **Right-to-Left Diagonals First** – Diagonal strokes angling right-to-left come before left-to-right diagonals (e.g., 人)

5. **Outside Before Inside** – Enclosing strokes are written before interior content (e.g., 同)

6. **Inside Before Outside** – Interior strokes precede outer enclosing strokes in certain structures (e.g., 凶)

7. **Inside Before Bottom Enclosing** – "Interior content is completed before the bottom closing stroke" (e.g., 因)

8. **Center Verticals Before Outside Wings** – Central strokes precede surrounding dots (e.g., 小)

9. **Cutting Strokes Last** – Vertical strokes cutting through a character are written after the horizontal strokes they cross (e.g., 中)

10. **Left Vertical Before Enclosing** – Leftmost vertical strokes precede enclosing strokes (e.g., 日)

11. **Top or Upper-Left Dots First** – Dots positioned at the top or upper-left are written earliest (e.g., 衣)

12. **Inside or Upper-Right Dots Last** – Dots in interior or upper-right positions are written last (e.g., 我)

These rules were naturally developed over centuries to promote writing efficiency and structural balance.
