# Kanji Stroke Order Rules & Principles

Core principles for writing kanji characters:

## Directional Rules

**Top to Bottom, Left to Right**: "When you write horizontal strokes, they go left to right. When you write vertical strokes, you go top to bottom."

**Horizontal Priority**: Most kanji should begin with horizontal strokes before vertical ones, starting from the upper-left area where initial strokes typically originate.

## Structural Principles

**Center Vertical Before Symmetrical Sides**: When a vertical line has symmetrical characters flanking it, write the central vertical stroke before the outer diagonal or curved strokes (though horizontal lines still come first).

**Box Formation**: Boxes use only three strokes—vertical first, then a horizontal stroke that continues as a right angle to the bottom, followed by a closing horizontal stroke from the left.

**Enclosure Priority**: When kanji contain enclosed spaces (like 回 or 国), complete three sides of the enclosure before filling the interior, then finish with the final enclosure stroke.

**Bottom Enclosure Last**: Bottom-side decorative elements come after all content above them.

## Diagonal & Special Cases

**Diagonal Ordering**: Right-to-left diagonals precede left-to-right ones, with upper-left diagonals written before lower ones.

**Through-Strokes Last**: Strokes crossing multiple parallel lines are saved for final placement.

**Dots & Dashes**: Minor marks typically come last, except when positioned at the very top.

## Complex Kanji

**Sectional Writing**: Complicated kanji are written component-by-component, progressing from top-left to bottom-right, applying rules fresh to each section.
