# Edward Johnston's Foundational Hand: Stroke Order & Ductus

Foundational hand is based on Johnston's studies of 9th and 10th century Carolingian script manuscripts.

## Core Stroke Principles

**Construction-Based Approach**: "Writing uses a repeatable sequence of strokes so the same letter can be made again tomorrow."

**7-Stroke Maximum Rule**: Letters should require no more than seven deliberate pen movements. Most letters need fewer strokes, encouraging efficient construction over patching weak shapes.

## Letter Family Progression

The Foundational hand teaches letters in families based on shared stroke characteristics:

1. **Round letters first**: o, c, e, a, d, g, q
   - The lowercase 'o' serves as the foundational test piece

2. **Straight-stem letters**: i, l, t

3. **Branched letters**: n, m, h, r

4. **Ascenders**: b, d, h, k, l

5. **Descenders**: p, q, g, y

## Detailed Lowercase 'O' Ductus

**Stroke Sequence with 30° Pen Angle:**

1. Set 30° pen angle consistently
2. Begin at upper right shoulder, just below x-height
3. Pull curved stroke down and left through the lower bowl
4. Keep curve moving through baseline (avoid flattening)
5. Close left and top curves back toward starting point
6. Maintain clean, centered counter with even visual weight

## Key Technique: Pen Angle Control

**Critical principle**: The 30° angle must remain constant throughout all strokes.

**Why this matters**: Rotating the nib to "rescue" letters ruins subsequent letterforms by creating inconsistent stroke weights.

## Historical Context

Edward Johnston (1872-1944) is regarded, along with Rudolf Koch, as the father of modern calligraphy, particularly for popularizing the broad-edged pen as a writing tool.

Johnston's foundational work "Writing & Illuminating & Lettering" (published 1908) remains the primary reference in the calligraphy domain, containing detailed instruction on stroke order and technique that calligraphers have studied for over a century.

Based on 9th-10th century Carolingian script, which was a fluent, cursive hand with fewer pen lifts than most historical scripts.
