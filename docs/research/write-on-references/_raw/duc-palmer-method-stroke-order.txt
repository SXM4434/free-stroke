# Palmer Method: Stroke Order and Letter Formation

The Palmer Method emphasizes muscular movement writing rather than finger manipulation, developed by Austin Palmer.

## Core Principles

**Muscular Movement**: "the arm is never raised above, but lies on the desk all the time in a perfectly natural, comfortable position."

**Speed Control**: Formation speed directly affects line quality. "Correct speed forces a light, firm line; too little speed results in shaky tremulous lines."

## Capital Letter Stroke Sequences

**A**: Begins with downward stroke, requires "a positive stop at the point of connection" at the top to prevent loops

**O**: Made with oval motion; "the final stroke should be pushed upward" to avoid resembling A

**C**: Pen "must move downward in the direction of a left curve" at the beginning stroke

**M & N**: Use the inverted figure six form for starting; finishing strokes drop below the baseline

## Lowercase Letter Stroke Sequences

**m & n**: Formed with "over-motion" at the tops; connective lines use "under-motion"

**o**: One-sixteenth inch height; requires angular stop at top closure

**l, b, h, k, f**: Loop letters extending equally above the baseline (four spaces high)

**d, g, q**: Share first parts with small 'a'

## Speed Requirements (Strokes per Minute)

- Compact ovals: 200 downward strokes per minute
- Capital A: 65-75 per minute
- Small o: 90+ per minute

Emphasizes that movement drills occur at specific rhythmic counts, establishing temporal consistency in formation.
